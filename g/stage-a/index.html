Stage A — Foundations

The ground everything else stands on: how a computer runs programs and controls access, what actually crosses the wire when two machines talk, the protocol every web attack and defense is built on, enough Python to read and change the small tools the field runs on, how to follow a vulnerability through source code, and what the cryptographic primitives do — and what breaks when they are misused.

Published 2026-10-09 · generated from the lesson data

The milestone

Explain, packet by packet, what happens between pressing “log in” and the page rendering: the DNS lookup, the TCP handshake, the TLS handshake, the HTTP request and response, the cookie or token that carries identity, and the bytes on the wire at each step.

What you can honestly claim

After Stage A you can honestly claim: nothing yet — and that is the point. This is the brief's own reasoning, not a verdict on your effort: foundations are prerequisites, not proof of skill. The checkable evidence starts in Stage B. What you can say is that you can explain the login-to-render path from first principles, with no term you cannot unpack.
